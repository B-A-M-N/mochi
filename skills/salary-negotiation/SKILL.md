# Salary Negotiation Procedure

## Trigger / when to use
Load when preparing to negotiate compensation: a raise, a promotion, a competing offer, or a new job offer where the number on the table is not final.

## Approach
1. **Know your market number first**: gather ranges from levels.fyi, Glassdoor, and peers for your level + location + stack. Negotiation without a market anchor is a mood, not a strategy.
2. **Define your walk-away and your ask**: the minimum you'd accept (walk-away) and the number that would make you excited (ask). Never negotiate against yourself — state the ask, then stop talking.
3. **Total compensation, not base**: equity, bonus, benefits, remote flexibility, and title all move. A lower base with meaningful equity can beat a higher base with none — model the whole package.
4. **Let them anchor first if possible; if you must, anchor high with justification**: "Based on market data for this level and the scope of the role, I'm targeting X." Justify with value, not need.
5. **Negotiate the package, not the person**: never bluff ("I have another offer" when you don't). Trade, don't threaten: "If base is fixed, could we revisit equity/signing/title?"

## Pitfalls
- Disclosing your current salary or your minimum — it caps your ceiling.
- Accepting the first number out of politeness — the first offer is rarely the best.
- Negotiating via email what should be a call; tone and silence matter.

## Verification
You can state your walk-away, your ask, and your market justification in one sentence each; the conversation ends with a written confirmation of the full package (base, bonus, equity, start date).
Check the final written offer against your walk-away number before signing.
