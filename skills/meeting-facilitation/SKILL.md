# Meeting Facilitation Procedure

## Trigger / when to use
Load when you are organizing a meeting, drafting an agenda, or asked to facilitate one — especially recurring ones that have drifted into "status readouts nobody acts on".

## Approach
1. **Define the decision, not the topic**: every agenda item states what will be decided or produced by the end. "Discuss X" is not an agenda item; "Decide X's owner and deadline" is.
2. **Timebox and sequence**: put the highest-stakes decision first (energy is highest), cap each item, and assign a timekeeper. A meeting without a timebox is a meeting that runs over.
3. **Default to async-first**: if an item only needs input, collect it before the meeting (a doc comment, a poll). Meeting time is for disagreement and decision, not information transfer.
4. **Facilitate in the room**: invite the quiet voices, park tangents on a visible "lot", and restate decisions + owners + deadlines before closing. Silence is not agreement — ask directly.
5. **Close the loop**: within the hour, send a short summary with decisions, owners, and due dates. A meeting without a written record did not happen.

## Pitfalls
- Letting the loudest person's framing become the decision frame.
- "Any other business" as a time sink — ban it or cap it at 2 minutes.
- Recurring meetings that outlive their purpose; kill or re-scope them quarterly.

## Verification
Every agenda item has a decision statement and a timebox; the follow-up message lists each decision with an owner and a due date; the next meeting starts from the previous action items.
