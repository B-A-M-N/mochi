# Cold Outreach Procedure

## Trigger / when to use
Load when writing cold emails for sales, partnerships, investor relations, hiring, or community building — any message to someone who did not ask to hear from you.

## Approach
1. **Subject line is the whole game**: 3–6 words, curiosity or relevance, no spam triggers ("Quick question", "Hello", "Opportunity"). The best subject references something specific to the recipient.
2. **First sentence is about them, not you**: prove you did homework — a specific observation about their work. Generic flattery is worse than none.
3. **One idea, one ask**: the body is 3–5 sentences max. State who you are (one line), why them (one line), and the single concrete ask (one line). Multiple asks get zero replies.
4. **Make the ask tiny and easy**: "15 minutes next week?" beats "Let's explore a partnership." Reduce the yes-cost to near zero.
5. **Follow up once, then stop**: a single follow-up 3–5 days later ("bumping this up") lifts reply rates dramatically. A third email is harassment.

## Pitfalls
- Attachments and links in the first email (spam filters + trust).
- "I know you're busy" — it signals you're about to waste their time.
- Writing a wall of text on mobile, where most cold email is read.

## Verification
The email fits one phone screen; the ask is a single yes/no with a default time; the first sentence could only be about this one recipient.
