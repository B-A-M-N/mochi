# Email Communication Procedure

## 1. Triage before replying
Sort every message into three piles: needs my action (with a deadline), waiting on someone else, and informational (archive after reading). Never reply in inbox order — answer by deadline, oldest first. If a message needs >5 minutes, reply with when you'll answer fully instead of going silent.

## 2. Lead with the bottom line
Put the ask or decision in the first two sentences (BLUF: bottom line up front), then context, then the exact action and date. Busy readers decide from the preview pane — burying the ask in paragraph three guarantees delay.

## 3. Match tone to relationship and stakes
Mirror the sender's formality one notch up for new contacts, match peers, and go warmer (never colder) under tension. Bad news gets a phone-call offer, never just text. Never send an angry draft the same day — write it, sleep on it, cut half.

## 4. Make every reply actionable
Each email ends with exactly one of: a decision made, a question with options and a default ("approve X unless I hear otherwise by Friday"), or a next step with an owner and date. Vague closings like "let me know your thoughts" restart the thread instead of ending it.

## 5. Pitfalls that burn professional capital
- Reply-all on anything emotional or corrective — default to direct reply, escalate the audience only deliberately.
- Committing dates you haven't checked against your calendar — verify first, promise second.
- Long threads past 3 exchanges — switch medium (call or doc) instead of writing email six.
- Forgetting follow-ups — every "waiting on someone" item gets a review date or it dies silently.

## 6. Verify before sending
Re-read once for: correct recipients, attachments actually attached, the ask and date present, tone appropriate for the most senior reader. For high-stakes mail, state the reversible parts explicitly so a misread costs nothing.
