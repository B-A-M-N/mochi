# Creative Writing Procedure

## Trigger / when to use
Load this skill when the user asks for stories, essays, marketing copy, scripts, or revisions of any non-code prose.

## 1. Lock the brief before drafting
Ask (or infer and state): audience, length, tone, and the one idea the reader must keep. A draft without a brief drifts — restate the brief in one line and get a nod before writing.

## 2. Structure first, sentences second
Outline the beats before drafting: hook, turn, payoff for stories; thesis, evidence, rebuttal, close for essays; headline, promise, proof, call-to-action for copy. Never draft into a blank page — rearrangement is cheaper than rewriting.

## 3. Draft fast in the target voice
Write the full draft without self-editing. Match voice with two anchors: sentence length (short punches, long flows) and diction (concrete nouns over abstractions). Read one paragraph aloud mentally — if you stumble, the reader will too.

## 4. Revise in passes, not loops
Pass 1 — structure: cut anything that doesn't serve the one idea. Pass 2 — clarity: one idea per sentence, active verbs, no throat-clearing openers. Pass 3 — sound: rhythm, repetition used on purpose, clichés replaced with specifics. Never mix passes; each pass has one job.

## 5. Pitfalls that ruin prose
- Opening with background instead of the hook — readers leave in 10 seconds.
- Hedging (`very`, `really`, `in order to`) — delete 90% on sight.
- Asking the reader to care before giving a reason — earn attention with specifics, then generalize.
- Ending with a summary of what was just said — end on the implication, not the recap.

## 6. Verify by reading the final aloud
Read the whole piece start to finish. Every stumble marks a sentence to fix. Confirm length is within 10% of the brief and the one idea survives in the first and last paragraph.
