# Personal Finance Review Procedure

## Trigger / when to use
Load when reviewing personal finances: building a budget, planning a big purchase, paying off debt, or just understanding where the money goes.

## Approach
1. **Audit before optimize**: 90 days of transactions, categorized. You cannot fix spending you haven't measured — the audit alone typically finds 10–20% in leaks.
2. **Build the floor first**: emergency fund (3–6 months of essential expenses) in a high-yield account before any investing or extra debt payoff. The floor prevents emergencies from becoming debt.
3. **Pay down high-interest debt aggressively**: anything above ~8% APR (credit cards) is an emergency. Avalanche (highest rate first) saves the most; snowball (smallest balance first) wins on motivation — pick the one you'll finish.
4. **Automate the good behavior**: savings, investments, and debt payments move on payday, before you can spend them. Willpower is a terrible financial instrument.
5. **Track the savings rate, not the budget**: the percentage of income you keep is the one number that predicts financial health. Budgets are a means; the rate is the end.

## Pitfalls
- Optimizing 1% subscriptions while ignoring the 30% housing/transport categories.
- Investing before the emergency fund and high-interest debt are handled.
- Lifestyle inflation that absorbs every raise — give your savings rate the raise instead.

## Verification
You can state your monthly essential expenses, your savings rate, and your debt payoff order from memory; the emergency fund is funded; every automated transfer fires on payday.
Run a 90-day spending audit and confirm every category is labeled before changing anything.
